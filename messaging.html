How To Use a Positioning Statement

This is internal — never ship it verbatim. It's the strategic brief every external-facing asset should trace back to.

  • Marketing: The buyer and needs blocks define who you're writing for. The compelling reason to buy sets the primary message hierarchy. The differentiation block limits what claims you're allowed to make.
  • Copywriting: Form 3 (headline/subheadline) is the starting point for website hero copy. Form 2 (problem/solution/outcome) maps to case studies and email sequences. Form 1 (single sentence) is the copy brief for ads, social, and event descriptions.
  • Product: Roadmap items that don't reinforce lane-level scoring or proactive surfacing are distractions. Every launch should reinforce, not reinvent, this narrative.
  • Sales: The buyer block filters ICP qualification. The needs block drives discovery questions. The compelling reason to buy is the answer to "so what does it do?" in the first meeting. The differentiation block arms reps for "how are you different from Power BI/Tableau?" conversations.
  • Keep it alive: Revisit this positioning when a major competitor makes a move, when new case study data lands, when a new ICP segment emerges, or at minimum once per year as part of GTM planning.
